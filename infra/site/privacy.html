Family Planner — privacy

How this app handles what a family puts into it.

Family Planner is a private app for one household. It is not a service: each family runs its own server, and there is no company behind it collecting anything.

What the server can read

Almost nothing. Events, lists, messages, photos, positions and saved passwords are encrypted on the phone that creates them, and only devices belonging to that family hold the keys. The server stores sealed blobs it cannot open, plus what it needs to deliver them: which device asked, when, and how large the item was.

What stays on the phone

  • The device's own identity key, in the phone's secure hardware store.
  • A decrypted copy of the family's content, for working offline.
  • Saved passwords, which are shown only after the phone confirms it is you — Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode.

Location

Sharing is off until a person turns it on, and each person chooses who sees them and how precisely. Only the latest position is kept — there is no trail, and no history to look back through. A position is reduced to its chosen precision on the sharer's phone, so "at school" leaves the device with no coordinates at all. A parent can ask a supervised child to share; the child's own screen says so plainly.

The few outside services involved

  • Map pictures come from OpenStreetMap, or Google Maps where a key is configured. That provider sees roughly which area is being looked at. It never receives who is where.
  • Weather comes from MET Norway. The position sent is deliberately blurred to about a kilometre first.
  • Notifications go through Firebase Cloud Messaging. The message carries no content — only "something changed, go and look" — so Google sees that a device was woken, never why.
  • Links you import (a recipe, a calendar feed) are fetched by the phone itself, from whatever site you gave it. The server never sees those addresses.

What there is none of

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no profiling, and no third party is sent anyone's personal data. Nothing is sold or shared, because there is nobody to sell it to.

Children

The app is meant to be used by a whole family, children included. A child's data is visible to their own family and to nobody else — not to the people who run the server software, who cannot read it either.

Keeping and deleting

Content stays until someone deletes it. Removing a device revokes its keys and moves the family's keys on, so that device cannot read anything new. A family that stops using the app can delete its server, and every copy goes with it.

Questions about this app and the data in it go to whoever runs your family's server — for this installation, that is the person who set it up.
